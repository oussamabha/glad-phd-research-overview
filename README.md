**Oussama BEN HAJ ALI**

## Research summary

This PhD develops a predictive, physics-based modeling framework for **Glancing Angle
Deposition (GLAD)** — a physical vapor deposition technique used to grow columnar,
highly porous thin-film nanostructures with tunable morphology.

The long-term objective is an interactive computational tool in which a user changes a
real deposition parameter (substrate tilt angle, rotation rate, vacuum pressure, source
distance, temperature) and obtains a live prediction of the resulting 3D nanostructure
morphology and downstream functional performance (optical response, gas-sensing
sensitivity, photovoltaic efficiency) — allowing fabrication parameters to be optimized
computationally before a physical deposition attempt.

The approach combines:
- Ballistic / Monte-Carlo deposition simulation of columnar growth
- Atomistic (molecular dynamics) calibration of surface diffusion and sticking behavior
- Physics-informed neural surrogate models (PINN / neural implicit fields) for fast
  morphology and property prediction
- Full-wave optical simulation and experimental ellipsometric validation

## Planned publications (in preparation)

- Ballistic GLAD void-fraction and column-tilt characterization across process parameters
- Statistical morphology inversion via correlated neural-implicit surrogate models
- Density-calibrated neural-implicit-field prediction of GLAD morphology
- Cross-material generalization of ballistic GLAD deposition models
- Atomistic anchoring and kinetic limits of surface diffusion in metal GLAD growth
- Full-wave polarimetric optical response of real 3D GLAD morphology
